01 · Nuance

How languages carve meaning

Can the Use of Derivatives Affect Cognition?

MA thesis · 2026

A Large-Scale Empirical Study on Morphological Derivation and the Korean Blue-Green Spectrum · Georg-August-Universität Göttingen · Supervisors: Prof. Dr. Stavros Skopeteas, Prof. Dr. Jacopo Romoli

Does language structure shape perception? Study 1 analyzes a curated corpus of 419 Korean color-term variants; Study 2 is a behavioral experiment across Korea, the US, the UK and Australia. Korean derivation is far more productive than English, and jamo-level similarity predicts semantic similarity better than morpheme-level similarity, partly via consonant strengthening independent of vowel-harmony shift.

  • 419 color-term variants
  • ≈ 21 : 1 KO vs EN unique forms
  • jamo > morpheme
  • N = 9,534 trials

Results fit a usage-salience account: frequent, morphologically economical categories are lexically compact and perceptually sharp. The design is correlational; the account is a plausible interpretation, not a demonstrated cause.

Light Verbs, Heavy Meaning?

Working paper · 2026

Exploring Informativity, Frequency and Morpho-Syntactic Interface of Simplex Verbs and 'root+hata' Light Verb Constructions in Korean · MA seminar, Göttingen

Tests Polinsky and Magyar's (2020) informativity account of head-final lexicons on Korean simplex verbs vs. root+hata constructions, using dictionary sense counts, embedding self-similarity (KLUE-BERT, KcBERT, Vertex AI) and corpus frequency.

  • 38 conditions
  • predicted asymmetry in 58%
  • item-driven, not structural

02 · Quantify

Measuring what people see and feel

The Grue Divide: When Cognitive Accuracy Doesn't Predict Affective Alignment

Poster · PANDORA @ EMNLP 2026

Sole author · Proceedings of the First Workshop on Pluralistic AI NLP (PANDORA 2026), pp. 35–68 · ACL · DOI 10.18653/2026.pandora-1.2

Claim
Cognitive accuracy does not guarantee affective alignment.
Evidence
Korean and Australian cohorts are statistically equivalent in error rate (TOST, p = 0.003). Yet in Sentiment–Formality–Friendliness (SFF) space, the Australian cohort clusters with the US and UK, away from Korea.
So what
A model optimized only for categorical accuracy can still miss culturally specific connotation. Alignment should model perceptual and socio-affective signal together.
  • 9,534 trials
  • ~2,200 took part
  • 1,159 analyzed
  • 4 cohorts: KR · US · UK · AU
  • SFF framework

Modalpartikeln: Can Sentiments Survive Translation with Emojis?

Term paper · 2024

Decoding Sentiments from German Modal Particles to Korean Using Emojis · The Meaning of Gestures (and more), Prof. Dr. Jacopo Romoli · featured on the DeepL Blog

Sehen wir and Sehen wir mal both come out as "Let's see", but the particle mal turns a businesslike decision into a relaxed suggestion. Two surveys measured this: German speakers rated the sentiment of modal particles, and Korean speakers rated expressive emojis. The two sets of scores matched at about 80%, with positive sentiment aligning almost exactly, suggesting emojis can carry tone that translation flattens.
